EMPIRICAL MASS FORMULAS IN PARTICLE PHYSICS — THE TRADITION
================================================================================

Last updated: 2026-04-19
Maintainer: A. Brilliant

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Koide formula is sometimes dismissed as "numerology." This document
establishes the broader tradition of empirical mass formulas in particle
physics, of which Koide is a legitimate and unexceptional member. Several
formulas in this tradition were ridiculed as coincidence when discovered
and later received rigorous dynamical explanations. Others remain
unexplained. The methodological point is that empirical mass formulas
have historically been productive inputs to fundamental physics, and a
claimed coincidence deserves a null test (which this project provides),
not reflexive dismissal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. CLASSIC EMPIRICAL FORMULAS THAT LATER GOT DYNAMICAL EXPLANATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## A1. Balmer series (1885)

Johann Balmer's formula for hydrogen spectral lines:

    1/lambda = R (1/n_1^2 - 1/n_2^2)

was purely empirical when discovered. Bohr's model (1913) provided the
dynamical explanation. This is the canonical example of a successful
empirical mass/energy formula: it quantitatively predicted the observed
spectrum with no theoretical framework behind it, and that predictive
success was what motivated the search for a framework.

Relevance: cited in Rivero-Gsponer (hep-ph/0505220) and many Koide
commentaries as the precedent for treating Koide seriously despite
lack of derivation.

## A2. Gell-Mann-Okubo mass formula (1961-62)

M. Gell-Mann and S. Okubo independently derived a sum rule for hadronic
multiplet masses:

    M = a_0 + a_1 Y + a_2 [ I(I+1) - Y^2/4 ]

where I is isospin and Y is hypercharge.

Citations:
    Gell-Mann, M., "The Eightfold Way", Caltech Synchrotron Lab
    Report CTSL-20 (1961). DOI: 10.2172/4008239.
    Okubo, S., Progress of Theoretical Physics 27, 949-966 (1962).

Status: The formula predicted the Omega-minus mass to within a few
percent and allowed its discovery. For pseudoscalar mesons, a
dynamical justification was later found in chiral perturbation theory
(they are pseudo-Goldstone bosons of dynamically broken chiral
symmetry). For the other multiplets the formula remains empirical but
is understood as a consequence of SU(3) flavor symmetry breaking.

Relevance to Koide: direct ancestor of "empirical mass formulas
eventually explained by symmetry." The Gell-Mann-Okubo case is
frequently cited as the template by which Koide might also eventually
be explained (e.g., by Sumino's family gauge symmetry).

## A3. Barut's lepton mass formula (1979)

A. O. Barut proposed an empirical formula connecting charged lepton
masses via a running-sum pattern:

    m_l(n) = m_e (1 + (3/2) alpha^(-1) sum_{k=0}^{n-1} k^4)

for n = 0, 1, 2. This generates m_mu to about 0.1% accuracy and m_tau
to within about 0.1-0.3%. Published in Physical Review Letters.

Citation:
    Barut, A. O., Physical Review Letters 42, 1251 (1979).

Status: remains empirical. Predates Koide by three years. Typically
cited alongside Koide as evidence that charged lepton masses fall into
unexpectedly simple patterns. Was similarly dismissed as coincidence
in the mainstream literature.

## A4. Harari-Haut-Weyers quark mass relation (1978)

H. Harari, H. Haut, and J. Weyers calculated theoretical values for
up, down, and strange quark masses from a model of Cabibbo mixing
that coincidentally reproduces a Koide-like structure (with m_u = 0).

Citation:
    Harari, H., Haut, H., Weyers, J.
    Physics Letters B 78, 459-461 (1978)
    DOI: 10.1016/0370-2693(78)90485-9

Status: predates Koide. Often cited as the first appearance of a
Koide-type formula in the literature, though the authors did not
frame it that way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. OTHER EMPIRICAL MASS FORMULAS (STILL UNEXPLAINED OR PARTIALLY EXPLAINED)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## B1. Regge trajectories

Empirically, hadron masses squared are linear in angular momentum J:

    J = alpha' M^2 + alpha_0

with a universal slope alpha' ~ 0.9 GeV^-2. This was purely empirical
in the 1960s; it is now understood in QCD via string-like flux-tube
dynamics, though a first-principles derivation remains incomplete.

## B2. Fritzsch texture ansatze (1970s-present)

H. Fritzsch and collaborators proposed Yukawa matrices with specific
texture-zero patterns that predict quark mass ratios and CKM angles.

Citation (foundational):
    Fritzsch, H., Physics Letters B 73, 317 (1978).

Status: ongoing active research; see Fritzsch-Xing review papers.
Many proposed texture ansatze fit observed masses and mixings; the
question of which texture is "correct" or whether any is dynamical
remains open.

## B3. Ramond-Roberts-Ross fermion mass patterns (1993)

    Ramond, P., Roberts, R. G., Ross, G. G.
    "Stitching the Yukawa quilt"
    Nuclear Physics B 406, 19 (1993)

Systematic classification of texture-zero fermion mass matrices
compatible with GUT assumptions. Partially explained by flavor
symmetries but the specific pattern remains empirical.

## B4. Froggatt-Nielsen flavor-hierarchy mechanism (1979)

    Froggatt, C. D., Nielsen, H. B.
    Nuclear Physics B 147, 277 (1979)

Derives fermion mass hierarchy from a spontaneously broken U(1)
flavor symmetry, where mass ratios are determined by charges under
the flavor symmetry. Semi-empirical: explains patterns of ratios
without predicting absolute masses.

## B5. Anarchy hypothesis (2000-present)

    Hall, L. J., Murayama, H., Weiner, N.
    "Neutrino mass anarchy"
    Physical Review Letters 84, 2572 (2000)
    arXiv:hep-ph/9911341

Proposes that neutrino Yukawa matrix entries are drawn uniformly from
a distribution with no preferred structure. Makes statistical
predictions for mixing angles via Monte Carlo; these have been
extensively tested (de Gouvêa-Murayama, Phys. Lett. B 573, 94 (2003);
arXiv:hep-ph/0301050). Methodologically relevant to our null test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. METHODOLOGICAL PRECEDENTS FOR NULL TESTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## C1. de Gouvêa-Murayama anarchy tests (2003, 2015)

    de Gouvêa, A., Murayama, H.
    "A statistical test of anarchy"
    Physics Letters B 573, 94 (2003), arXiv:hep-ph/0301050

    de Gouvêa, A., Murayama, H.
    "Neutrino mixing anarchy: alive and kicking"
    Physics Letters B 747, 479 (2015), arXiv:1204.1249

These papers perform the exact style of test we are doing: fix an
algorithm, draw random inputs from a prior, compute tail probabilities
for observed values. Their 2015 update finds P(anarchy gives values
at least as improbable as observed) = 44%-64%, i.e., anarchy survives.

This is the direct methodological ancestor of our Koide null test.

## C2. Donoghue random-quark-mass program (1998-present)

    Donoghue, J. F., "The weight for random quark masses"
    Physical Review D 57, 5499 (1998), arXiv:hep-ph/9712333

    Donoghue, J. F., Dutta, K., Ross, A.
    "Quark and lepton masses and mixing in the landscape"
    Physical Review D 73, 113002 (2006), arXiv:hep-ph/0511219

Tests whether observed fermion masses are consistent with a
scale-invariant random distribution. Establishes the log-uniform
prior as phenomenologically supported.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. THE CASE FOR TAKING EMPIRICAL MASS FORMULAS SERIOUSLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The empirical discovery of quantitative patterns in particle spectra
has historically been productive:

1. Balmer -> Bohr -> quantum mechanics.
2. Mendeleev's periodic table -> atomic shell structure.
3. Gell-Mann-Okubo -> SU(3) flavor symmetry.
4. Regge trajectories -> string theory -> QCD flux tubes.
5. Cabibbo angle pattern -> CKM matrix -> three-generation structure.

In each case, an empirical formula or pattern was discovered first and
a dynamical explanation came later — sometimes decades later. The
discovery of the pattern motivated the search for the framework.

The Koide formula has held for 40+ years across a factor of 35
improvement in tau-mass measurement precision. It makes a specific
numerical prediction (m_tau = 1776.97 MeV) that has survived all
subsequent experimental refinements. This pattern of behavior —
precise, prediction-making, persistent across measurement
improvements — is the signature of a formula that reflects
something real, not a coincidence.

Whether that "something real" is (a) Sumino's family gauge symmetry,
(b) a geometric structure (this work, via Descartes-Soddy),
(c) some connection to Yukawa landscape statistics (Donoghue),
or (d) something not yet considered, is a live question. The
null-hypothesis Monte Carlo test in this project quantifies the
degree to which the pattern is statistically unusual under the
best-motivated "accident" hypothesis (the Donoghue log-uniform
prior), which is the minimum evidentiary bar required to justify
further theoretical investment.
